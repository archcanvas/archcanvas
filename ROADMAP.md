# Roadmap

What ArchCanvas is working on, in the open.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Nothing here is a commitment or a date.** This is one person's ordered guess, and it changes when
> the evidence does — usually because of something someone reported here. Items move up the list when
> people ask for them; the fastest way to influence it is to
> [open a discussion](https://github.com/archcanvas/archcanvas/discussions) or 👍 an existing issue.

---

## Now

**Design quality is the whole game.** Everything else is secondary until a plan looks like something
a person who knows buildings would have drawn.

- **Proportion and variety.** The current designer is measurably sound — rooms are reachable,
  dimensions hold — and still too fond of the rectangle. Getting better proportions and less
  sameness across briefs is the active work.
- **Fewer things named on the drawing.** When the design can't satisfy part of a brief, it says so on
  the plan. That list should get shorter because the plans get better, not because the checks get
  quieter.
- **Watching the new designer in the wild.** It went live at the start of September; the next change
  is informed by what real briefs do to it, not by more internal testing.

## Next

- **Speed.** A generation takes about 100–160 seconds today. That trade is deliberate, but the number
  is not sacred.
- **More control over the fit-out.** Furniture placement is automatic; being able to say "put the
  wardrobe on the other wall" and have it stick is a common ask.
- **Better handling of a real site.** Slopes, awkward plots, existing walls to keep — the cases where
  "describe your build" currently loses information.

## Under consideration

Ideas that are real but unscheduled. If one of these matters to you, say so — that is exactly the
signal that moves it.

- Imperial units alongside metric.
- An area schedule exported with the plan.
- Editing from a phone (today the canvas is comfortable on a laptop and cramped on a phone).
- Comments on a share link, so a client can point at the plan rather than describe the spot.
- More building typologies beyond the current presets.
- Rendering styles — the current renderings have one look.

## Not planned

Being explicit about these saves everyone time.

- **Permit-ready or construction documents.** ArchCanvas produces a concept plan to take *to* a
  professional. Making it look like a permit set would be dishonest about what has actually been
  checked. See [what it is not](README.md#what-it-is-not--honestly).
- **Structural, services or code-compliance checking.** Same reason.
- **A subscription.** You design a house a handful of times in your life, not every month.
- **Open-sourcing the application.** The substrate is open — that's
  [ArchLang](https://github.com/ChanMeng666/archlang), MIT, and it draws every plan you see here. The
  product on top of it is not, and won't be.
